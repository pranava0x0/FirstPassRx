## 1. The incumbent rails: how ePA actually flows today

### NCPDP SCRIPT ePA (the pharmacy-benefit standard)

- Pharmacy-benefit ePA runs on the **NCPDP SCRIPT standard** (ePA transactions added in SCRIPT v10.6, July 2013). Core flow: prescriber's system sends a PA initiation/request; the **PBM returns a structured response containing its own question set** (required data fields + clinical questions) embedded in the SCRIPT message — *not* a free-text form. The prescriber's system renders the payer's questions, the answers go back as a structured PARequest, and the PBM replies approve / deny / pend. Source: https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ncpdp-script-epa-surescripts (accessed 2026-08-24).
- Implication: in true ePA, **the payer controls the form**. The "form" is a dynamic question set served per-drug/per-patient by the PBM at transaction time. Anyone "pre-drafting a PA form" outside this rail is really pre-drafting either (a) the fax/portal PDF version of the payer's criteria, or (b) the *answers* the prescriber will need regardless of channel.
- Industry is mid-upgrade to **NCPDP SCRIPT v2023011** (mandatory 2028-01-01; Surescripts made it available to early adopters across ePA as of early 2026). Source: https://surescripts.com/insights/navigating-eprescribing-standards-upgrades (accessed 2026-08-24).
- Regulatory note: **CMS-0057-F (the Jan 2024 interoperability/PA final rule) excludes drug PA** — its FHIR PA APIs (payer compliance by Jan 2027) cover medical-benefit services, not pharmacy-benefit drugs. Pharmacy ePA stays on NCPDP rails. Source: https://www.insighthealth.ai/blog/ai-prior-authorization-software and https://rxalmanac.com/articles/ai-prior-auth-vendors/ (accessed 2026-08-24).

### Surescripts (CompletEPA)

- Dominant e-prescribing network; ~30.5B health transactions/yr (2025 figure). **CompletEPA** is its ePA product, embedded in EHRs (Epic, Oracle Health/Cerner, etc.), leveraging Surescripts eligibility + formulary data to *prospectively* flag PA-required drugs inside e-prescribing; also runs a standalone web Prior Authorization Portal. By 2017 CompletEPA already reached ~75% of physicians' EHRs and ~85% of PBM lives. Source: https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ncpdp-script-epa-surescripts (accessed 2026-08-24).
- Publicly supportive of CMS efforts to streamline PA for prescribers: https://surescripts.com/news-center/intelligence-in-action/medication-affordability/surescripts-supports-cms-in-its-effort-to-streamline-prior-authorization-for-prescribers-and-expedite-patient-access-to-needed-medications (accessed 2026-08-24).

### CoverMyMeds (McKesson)

- The other rail. Network: ~75% of EHRs ("500+ EHR integrations"), 50,000+ pharmacies, ~950,000 providers, payers representing ~95% of prescription volume. Built on NCPDP SCRIPT; platform identifies the patient's PBM, matches the correct question set/form, routes the request, returns approve/deny/pend. Falls back to fax when a payer lacks a live ePA endpoint. Sources: https://www.covermymeds.health/our-solutions/prior-authorization ; https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/electronic-prior-authorization-platforms (accessed 2026-08-24).
- **Business model: free to providers and pharmacies; funded by pharma and PBMs.** This is why it won the network game. Claims: up to 3x faster than phone/fax, therapy starts up to 13 days sooner, 62% of electronic requests decided within 2 hours. Source: https://rxalmanac.com/vendor/covermymeds/ ; https://www.covermymeds.health/ (accessed 2026-08-24).
- 2025 expansion: acquired **RxLightning** (digital specialty-med enrollment) and **FastAuth** (automated end-to-end patient-access software). Source: search summary via https://rxalmanac.com/vendor/covermymeds/ (accessed 2026-08-24; acquisition details UNVERIFIED beyond secondary source).

### Availity

- Primarily a medical-benefit clearinghouse/portal (multi-payer provider portal). Its PA AI play is **Availity AuthAI** — a *payer-side* product: authorization recommendations in <90 seconds, "transparent, auditable" logic, positioned for CMS-0057 compliance (medical benefit). Not a pharmacy-ePA drafting tool for prescribers. Source: https://healthaidaily.com/blog/best-ai-prior-authorization-software-small-practices-2026/ (accessed 2026-08-24).

### The sobering adoption evidence (why the rails haven't killed the pain)

- Despite near-universal availability, **~49-50% of PA requests were still phone/fax** (2019-2020 data); among ePA-enabled providers ~50% still revert to manual for some PAs.
- 2022 survey (n=1,147 providers): 58% had used ePA software, yet 82% still spent up to 5 hrs/week on PA; ePA users spent *more* total time (they handle more volume); time-to-decision no shorter overall (though payer decisions returned faster, p=0.004).
- Sutter Health analysis of 74,000+ ePA prescriptions: no adherence improvement; average fill delay 4.2 days for ePA vs 3.0 days traditional.
- All from: https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ncpdp-script-epa-surescripts (accessed 2026-08-24).
- Takeaway: the *transport* is solved; the *drafting/answering burden* (pulling chart data, matching criteria language, writing medical-necessity narratives) is what everyone still pays for — that is the layer the AI startups attack, and the layer a FirstPassRx drafting feature would touch.

## 5. Key dynamics

### 5a. Where the payer's question set / criteria / forms come from

Five distinct sourcing strategies observed (access 2026-08-24):
1. **The PBM serves it live (true ePA):** in NCPDP SCRIPT ePA the question set is returned by the PBM per-transaction; CoverMyMeds/Surescripts render it. No scraping involved; the network relationship IS the product. Source: https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ncpdp-script-epa-surescripts
2. **Manually curated form libraries:** MMIT (Norstella) keeps "a dedicated team that manually sources, vets, builds and maintains a repository of prior authorization and medical exception forms," embedded into its CoverageFinder product and sold to pharma. This is the enterprise-priced version of "collect all the payer PDFs." Source: https://www.mmitnetwork.com/prior-authorization-medical-exception-form-inclusions/ ; https://www.mmitnetwork.com/coveragefinder/
3. **Browser automation of payer portals:** Honey Health operates payer portals directly via browser automation (no partnership needed). Source: https://www.honeyhealth.ai/articles/10-best-ai-specialty-drug-prior-authorization-tools-2026
4. **Payer partnership / policy encoding (payer-side vendors):** Cohere, Anterior, Banjo, Basys encode the payer's own criteria (Banjo: "clinical criteria into decision trees") under contract with the plan/PBM. Sources: https://www.banjohealth.com/ ; https://basys.ai/
5. **AI parsing of public payer policy documents:** Tennr's RaeLM interprets records "against complex payor criteria" assembled from payer documentation; provider-side startups generally maintain scraped/curated policy corpora. Source: https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/tennr-clinches-101m-build-out-ai-automates-patient-referral-workflows
- FirstPassRx-relevant: strategies 2 and 5 need no permission — payer PA forms and criteria documents are public PDFs, and FirstPassRx already archives sources with provenance (`sources/manifest.json` pattern).

### 5b. Published evidence on AI-drafted PA quality/approval

- **arXiv 2603.29366 (Awan & Raza, 2026-03-31), "AI-Generated Prior Authorization Letters: Strong Clinical Content, Weak Administrative Scaffolding"** — 3 commercial LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) x 45 physician-validated scenarios across 5 specialties. Findings: clinical content >97% mean scores (accurate dx, solid medical-necessity arguments, thorough step-therapy documentation; Claude highest at 11.98/12); **administrative elements systematically missing — billing codes in only 0-51% of letters, authorization duration 0-27%, follow-up plans 7-27%**; zero clinical hallucinations across 135 letters (with structured prompts). Conclusion: winning systems need a **deterministic layer of structured formulary/billing/payer data wrapped around the LLM narrative**; supervised-review workflow cuts per-letter time from 20-30 min to 5-10 min. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29366 (accessed 2026-08-24).
- Vendor-reported numbers (not peer-reviewed): PrescriberPoint 94.5% clinician acceptance + 86% approval (sample); Counterforce 70% appeal-reversal; Anterior 99.24% clinical accuracy + 76% auto-approval lift (payer-side); Latent case studies (MetroHealth PA turnaround 1-2 months → ~2 weeks). All cited in sections above.
- Systemic evidence that transport alone doesn't save time (sec 1: ePA users spend MORE total time; Sutter 4.2-day vs 3.0-day fill delay) — the value is in criteria-matching and first-pass completeness, not the pipe.

### 5c. Business-model pattern

- **Free-to-provider, pharma/PBM-funded is the dominant GTM for drug PA**: CoverMyMeds (pharma+PBM funded), SamaCare (pharma-sponsored portal), Forus/Tandem (free to providers/patients, 5 of top-10 biopharma pay; $1B valuation), PrescriberPoint (pharma microsite subscriptions). Providers have near-zero willingness to pay; pharma pays for pull-through, payers pay for review automation.
- Where providers DO pay: per-PA pricing exists at ~$1.50-2/completed PA (Honey Health) or enterprise SaaS for health systems (Latent, Humata, Valer).
- Consolidation: Myndshft→DrFirst (2024), Glidian→Infinx (2025), RxLightning+FastAuth→CoverMyMeds (2025), MajorBoost→SuperDial (2025). Independent point solutions are getting absorbed into rails/EHR vendors.

## Implications for FirstPassRx

1. **The exact niche is empty.** Free layer today = patient appeal letters (Claimable/FHI/Counterforce, post-denial) + generic clinician drafting (Doximity GPT, no payer awareness) + one pharma-funded payer-aware drafter (PrescriberPoint — accounts, EHR/chart upload, PHI). **Zero-login, zero-PHI, static, payer-criteria-aware PA *preparation*** is unoccupied.
2. **FirstPassRx already owns the scarce half.** The arXiv study's punchline: LLM clinical narrative is commoditized; what's missing is the **deterministic administrative/payer scaffolding** — exact form, criteria checklist, step-therapy expectations, auth-duration norms, fax numbers, formulary citations. That's structured curation work, which is FirstPassRx's existing muscle (per-state, per-payer, per-class, cited). Build the scaffolding, let the prescriber (or their Doximity GPT) write the narrative.
3. **Never rebuild the rails.** Submission, status-tracking, and the live PBM question set are network businesses (CoverMyMeds/Surescripts are already free to providers). The right boundary: produce a **draft/prep pack** (payer's actual form PDF + pre-filled drug/criteria fields + criteria-answer checklist + LMN skeleton) and hand off — print, download, or link out. No accounts, no fax gateway, no PHI ever server-side. Anything patient-specific gets typed locally by the office (client-side only).
4. **Target the fax/portal half of the market.** ~50% of drug PAs still run phone/fax/portal, concentrated in exactly FirstPassRx's audience: small practices without deep EHR ePA enablement. In true ePA the question set is dynamic and payer-served, so a static pre-draft should aim at the payer's *published* fax/portal form + the criteria the ePA questions will mirror anyway (prepping answers has value in both channels).
5. **Form sourcing is legal, public, and already half-built.** Medicaid FFS/MCO PA forms and criteria are public PDFs; MMIT charges pharma enterprise prices for a hand-maintained library of the same documents. FirstPassRx's `archive-sources` pipeline (URL + sha256 + capture date) is the free, auditable version — scoped to the states/classes/payers already in the dataset, drift-detected like everything else.
6. **Stay neutral — it's the differentiator.** Every free competitor with payer-aware drafting is pharma-funded (PrescriberPoint, SamaCare, Forus), which structurally biases toward sponsored brands. FirstPassRx's "cheapest compliant path first, cash price when it beats insurance" stance is the un-conflicted counter-position; keep it and say so.
7. **Appeals are a solved free problem — link, don't build.** If a user's PA is denied, point to Claimable/Counterforce/FHI rather than building appeal generation. The pre-denial FirstPassRx move ("pick the preferred agent, or submit a criteria-complete PA the first time") is upstream of all of them.
8. **Scope discipline: drafting ≠ predicting.** Don't claim approval odds (vendors' 86%/94% numbers come from submission platforms with feedback loops FirstPassRx won't have). Frame output as "this payer's published criteria, pre-organized" — facts with citations, consistent with the data-handling rules already in CLAUDE.md (facts/estimates/judgments in separate lanes).
9. **Watch list for gap-closure:** Latent ($80M, health systems), Forus ($1B, pharma-funded free), Develop Health (pharmacy-benefit, telehealth), Honey Health ($1.50-2/PA — cheap enough for small practices), and CoverMyMeds itself (RxLightning/FastAuth acquisitions push toward auto-drafting). If any ships a no-login free tier for small practices, the static niche narrows to "no-PHI + state-Medicaid depth + neutrality."
10. **Regulatory tailwind is medical-benefit only.** CMS-0057-F's FHIR PA APIs (2027) exclude drugs; pharmacy PA stays on NCPDP + fax/portal indefinitely — the messy channel FirstPassRx targets isn't being modernized away any time soon.
